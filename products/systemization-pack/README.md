## Module 1 — Retainer Conversion Script (complete)

### When to use it

Send this within 48 hours of delivering a one-off project that went well — momentum is highest right after the client has seen the finished work, before they've moved on to the next thing.

### The email

> Subject: Keeping things running after [project]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Glad [project] landed well. A few clients in a similar spot have asked me to stay on for ongoing work — [maintenance / monthly updates / a recurring batch of the same deliverable] — instead of starting from scratch each time they need something.
>
> If that's useful, I have a retainer option: [X hours / X deliverables] per month for $[Y], billed on the 1st, with priority turnaround over one-off requests. It's the same rate as project work, just smoothed out so neither of us is re-negotiating scope every time.
>
> No pressure either way — happy to keep working project-by-project if that fits better. Let me know what makes sense for you.

### Why this framing works

- It's offered as a convenience to the client (less re-explaining context, priority turnaround), not as you asking for stability — even though that's exactly what it gives you.
- The price is anchored to your existing project rate, so it doesn't feel like a new negotiation.
- The opt-out ("no pressure, happy to keep working project-by-project") removes the pressure that makes clients say no on reflex.

### Pricing the retainer

Take your average monthly revenue from that client over their last 2-3 projects, divide by the hours it actually took you, and price the retainer at that hourly-equivalent times a 10-20% smoothing premium — you're trading the client's flexibility to call you anytime for your loss of the ability to fill that capacity with a different, possibly better-paying client.
