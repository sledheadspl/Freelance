# Client-Screening Checklist

Run this before you send a proposal, not after you've already started. Each "yes" is a real warning — three or more and you should price for the risk or pass entirely.

## Money signals

- [ ] They led with "what's your cheapest option" before describing the project
- [ ] They compared your rate to a much lower quote unprompted
- [ ] They balked at a deposit, or asked to pay 100% on completion
- [ ] "Budget" was described as "flexible" with no number attached
- [ ] They've already gone through 2+ freelancers for this same project

## Scope signals

- [ ] The brief changed more than once before any contract was signed
- [ ] "It's a small job" was said about something with no clear end state
- [ ] They used the words "just," "quick," or "simple" to describe multi-step work
- [ ] There's no single decision-maker — multiple stakeholders with different opinions
- [ ] They want to "see how it goes" before agreeing to scope or price

## Working-relationship signals

- [ ] Response time was already slow or erratic during the proposal stage
- [ ] They mentioned a "huge opportunity" tied to working below your normal rate
- [ ] You were asked to start before paperwork or deposit cleared
- [ ] They speak about past freelancers as universally incompetent or difficult
- [ ] Your gut said no and you're rationalizing past it because you need the cash

## Scoring

- **0–1 checked:** normal client, proceed with your standard proposal.
- **2–3 checked:** proceed, but price it up 15–25% and tighten the exclusions list in your proposal — you're pricing in the friction you can already see.
- **4+ checked:** this client is statistically likely to produce scope creep, late payment, or both. Either price high enough to make the risk worth it, or pass. A client you turn down can't generate a single hour of unbillable conflict next month.
