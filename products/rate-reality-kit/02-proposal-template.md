# Value-Based Proposal Template

Copy this structure. Replace every `[bracket]`. Delete this line before sending.

---

**Project: [Client Name] — [One-line outcome, not deliverable]**
*e.g. "Cut [Client]'s support response time from 2 days to 2 hours" — not "Build a help desk."*

### The situation

[1-2 sentences restating the client's problem in their own words, proving you listened. No fluff.]

### What changes when this is done

- [Concrete outcome 1 — tied to time, money, or risk the client cares about]
- [Concrete outcome 2]
- [Concrete outcome 3]

### The plan

| Package | What's included | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Essentials** — $[X] | [Smallest version that still solves the core problem] | [Outcome] |
| **Standard** — $[X × 1.6–2] | [Essentials + the next-most-requested addition] | [Outcome] |
| **Premium** — $[X × 2.5–3] | [Standard + priority turnaround / extra deliverable / ongoing support] | [Outcome] |

*Most clients land on Standard. List it in the middle, never first.*

### Timeline

[Start date] → [delivery date]. [One sentence on what you need from the client to hit this — assets, access, approvals — stated up front so delays are visibly on them, not you.]

### What's not included

[1-3 explicit exclusions — e.g. "Does not include ongoing hosting," "Revisions beyond round 2 billed at $[rate]/hr." This is what the scope-creep scripts in this kit will point back to.]

### Next step

Reply "approved" or sign below, and I'll send the contract + deposit invoice ([X]% to start, balance on delivery).

---

**Why this works:** the price is anchored to outcomes before the client ever sees a number, the three-tier table does the upselling for you, and the exclusions list is the fence you'll point to later instead of arguing scope in the moment.
