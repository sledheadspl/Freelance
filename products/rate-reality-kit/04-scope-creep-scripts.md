# Scope-Creep Scripts

Send these as close to verbatim as fits your voice. The goal of every script below is the same: acknowledge the request, point to the agreed scope, and give a path forward that doesn't cost you margin.

## "While you're in there, can you also—"

> Happy to take a look at that. It's outside what we scoped in the proposal ([link/reference]), so I'll put together a quick add-on quote and timeline impact before starting — want me to send that over?

## A "small" request that's actually a new feature

> That's a good addition, but it's a different piece of work from what we scoped — more like a [days/hours] add than a tweak. I can fit it into this round of work for $[X] / [X hours], or scope it as phase two once the current deliverable ships. Which do you prefer?

## Client says "I thought this was included"

> I get why it might've seemed that way — to be clear on my end, the proposal scoped [restate original scope in one line], and this falls outside that. I can add it for $[X], or we keep it for a future round. No issue either way, just want to make sure we're tracking the same scope going forward.

## Repeated small asks adding up

> I've noticed a few requests outside the original scope over the past [timeframe] — individually small, but they add up to about [X hours/days] of extra work. I want to keep delivering well for you, so going forward I'll quote anything outside the original scope before starting, even small items. Sound fair?

## Client pushes back on a scope-add quote

> Totally understandable. If it doesn't make sense to add right now, we can ship the current scope as-is and revisit this as its own project later — happy to make a note of it so it doesn't get lost.

## Deadline pressure used to justify free scope creep

> I want to hit your deadline, and the fastest way to do that is to keep this round scoped exactly as agreed — anything additional right now would push the date out rather than pull it in. I can quote the extra piece for after delivery if that works.

---

**The pattern in all six:** never argue that the client is wrong to ask. Name the gap between the request and the agreed scope, then hand them a priced choice. You're not the bad guy for charging more — you're the only adult in the conversation tracking what was actually agreed.
